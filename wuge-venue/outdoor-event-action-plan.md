# Wuge Venue — Outdoor Event Action Plan

Goal: get the open space ready to host outdoor events (stage, food trucks, restrooms, fenced area) so that a production company like Livewire can arrive, set up, and run a show without having to figure anything out on site.

Status: draft, 2026-10-27. Nothing has been measured yet, so every number below is a placeholder until the site walk is done.

---

## 1. Capacity: the formula (fill it in after the site walk)

How many people fit is decided by several limits, not just square footage. Calculate each one and use the **lowest**.

| Limit | How to calculate it | Your number |
|---|---|---|
| **Floor area** | Usable sq ft ÷ sq ft per person (see the density table) | |
| **Exits** | Total gate width in inches ÷ 0.2 in per person (check this factor with the fire marshal) | |
| **Restrooms** | Number of portable toilets × about 100 people | |
| **Parking** | Parking spaces × 2.5 people per car | |
| **Fire marshal / permit** | The number the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) signs off on | |

**Usable sq ft** = total fenced area − stage and wings − FOH/production − food truck lane − restroom bank − aisles and buffers. A good starting assumption is to take 25–35% off the gross area.

**Density table** (standard planning figures; NFPA 101 uses 5 sq ft net for standing space):

| Crowd type | Sq ft per person |
|---|---|
| Packed standing, concert pit (legal maximum) | 5 |
| Standing with room to move | 7 |
| Comfortable festival with blankets or chairs | 10–15 |

**Worked example (made-up numbers, for illustration only):** 1 acre (43,560 sq ft) − 30% = about 30,500 usable sq ft → about 3,000 people at 10 sq ft each, or about 6,100 at 5 sq ft each. In practice parking or restrooms usually become the limit long before floor area does.

## 2. Action items

### Phase 1: Measure and document (do this first)
- [ ] Walk the site with a measuring wheel or GPS app and record the length and width of the open area.
- [ ] Get an aerial photo or drone shot and mark north, slope, low and wet spots, and overhead lines.
- [ ] Find power: where it is, how many amps and what phase, and how far it is from the likely stage spot. If there is no shore power, plan on generators.
- [ ] Find water access, for food trucks and handwash stations.
- [ ] Check ground conditions: can it take a box truck, a semi, or a mobile stage trailer when the ground is wet?
- [ ] Confirm sight lines from the planned stage spot. Put the stage so the sun is behind the audience at show time and sound projects away from the neighbors.
- [ ] Count parking and identify overflow parking.

### Phase 2: Build the venue tech pack (this is what makes Livewire's job easy)
- [ ] One-page site map with these zones marked: stage, FOH, load-in route, food truck lane, restroom bank, entry and exit gates, emergency lane, parking, green room.
- [ ] Load-in specifications: gate width, turning radius, distance from truck to stage, surface type.
- [ ] Power specifications: location, amperage, connector types, and generator staging area.
- [ ] Contact sheet: site lead, local fire, police, EMS, utilities.
- [ ] Send the pack to Livewire and ask them to mark it up with their stage footprint, power draw, crew size, and setup and strike times.

### Phase 3: Permits and compliance (start early, as some approvals take 30–90 days)
- [ ] Call the local township or city about a special event / mass gathering permit.
- [ ] Fire marshal: occupancy number, egress, and stage and tent approval.
- [ ] Health department: food truck licensing and handwash requirements.
- [ ] Liquor license or catering permit, if alcohol will be served.
- [ ] Noise ordinance and curfew hours.
- [ ] Insurance: event liability policy, plus a certificate of insurance from every vendor (Livewire, food trucks, toilets, fencing) naming the property as additional insured. **Have your insurance agent and an attorney review the policy and vendor agreements.**

### Phase 4: Vendor lineup (get two to three quotes for each)
- [ ] **Staging and production:** Livewire, covering stage, sound, lighting, and power.
- [ ] **Portable toilets:** about 1 per 100 attendees for a 4–6 hour event; add 20–30% if alcohol is served; at least 1 ADA unit per 20 units (and never zero); about 1 handwash station per 10 units.
- [ ] **Fencing:** quoted by the linear foot (perimeter length from Phase 1). Panels are usually 6 ft tall and 10–12 ft wide. Include entry and exit gates, emergency gates, and windscreen if the site is windy.
- [ ] **Food trucks:** set terms (a flat fee, a percentage of sales, or both), power and water needs, and a waste plan.
- [ ] **Security and EMS:** bag check at the entry, crowd control, and a first aid station.
- [ ] **Trash and recycling:** dumpsters and a cleanup crew.
- [ ] **Lighting** for parking and paths after dark.

### Phase 5: Operating plan
- [ ] Weather plan: lightning and wind thresholds, who makes the call to stop the show, and the evacuation route.
- [ ] Timeline: load-in, doors, show, strike, load-out.
- [ ] Day-of roles, with one person accountable for each area: gate, vendors, production liaison, safety.

## 3. Livewire: what to ask on the first call

Livewire ([livewirenow.com](https://livewirenow.com/), info@livewirenow.com) is a full-service event production company. Listings say it handles staging, rigging, power distribution, sound, lighting, video and on-site logistics. Its listings show both Minnesota and Fargo, ND addresses, so confirm which office would serve Wuge.

- [ ] Which stage do they recommend for our crowd size? Ask for its footprint (width × depth), clearance height, and wing space.
- [ ] Do they bring their own generators and power distribution? What is the total power draw, and how much fuel will it need?
- [ ] What does the truck need to get in: how many trucks and how large, the turning radius, and whether the ground needs mats or a hard surface?
- [ ] How long are setup and strike, how many crew, and when do they need site access?
- [ ] What is their wind limit, and who makes the weather hold call?
- [ ] Will a licensed electrician be on site? Can they provide engineering or load ratings for the stage and rigging? The fire marshal may ask for these.
- [ ] Can they send a certificate of insurance naming the property as additional insured?
- [ ] What pricing do they offer: a full-package day rate, or a recurring or season rate if we run a series?
- [ ] After a site walk, will they mark up our site map with where the stage and FOH should go?

---

## 4. Questions to answer first
1. What event are we designing for: one concert, a recurring series, or renting the space to outside promoters?
2. Will alcohol be served? It affects permits, insurance, security, and restroom counts.
3. What is the revenue model: ticketing, venue rental fee, food truck fees, sponsorships? Get a target per event before spending on infrastructure.
4. Is the open space zoned for this use?
